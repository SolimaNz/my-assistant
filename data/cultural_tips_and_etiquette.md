# Egyptian Cultural Tips and Etiquette

## Tipping Culture (البقشيش)
Tipping is deeply embedded in Egyptian culture. Almost every service interaction involves a small tip (but only if you want it's not obligatory).

### Standard Tipping Guide
- **Restaurants:** 10–15% of the bill. Check if service charge is already included (often 12% is added but doesn't go to the waiter — tip on top).
- **Hotel porters:** 20–50 EGP per bag.
- **Hotel housekeeping:** 30–50 EGP per day, left on the pillow.
- **Taxi drivers:** Round up or add 10–20 EGP.
- **Uber/Careem drivers:** Tip through the app or 10–20 EGP cash.
- **Bathroom attendants:** 5–10 EGP.
- **Parking attendants (سايس):** 10–20 EGP. They appear at almost every parking spot.
- **Tour guides (full day):** 200–500 EGP depending on quality.
- **Felucca captains:** 50–100 EGP per person for a short ride.
- **Gas station attendants:** 5–10 EGP.
- **Grocery baggers:** 5–10 EGP.
- **Anyone who "helps" you at a monument:** Be cautious — if you didn't ask for help, you can politely decline. If you accept, 20–50 EGP is reasonable.

### Important Tipping Notes
- Always carry small bills (5, 10, 20, 50 EGP notes). Getting change is difficult.
- "Baksheesh" (بقشيش) is expected, not optional. Budget for it.
- Some people will offer unsolicited "services" (opening doors, pointing directions) and expect a tip. A polite "la shukran" (لا شكرًا, no thank you) works.

## Bargaining and Shopping

### Where to Bargain
- Khan El-Khalili and all souqs/markets
- Street vendors
- Small shops without price tags
- Taxi fares (with black taxis only, not white metered ones)

### Where NOT to Bargain
- Supermarkets and chain stores (Carrefour, Spinneys, etc.)
- Restaurants and cafes
- Pharmacies
- Government services and museums (fixed ticket prices)
- Uber/Careem (prices are app-set)

### Bargaining Tips
1. Start at 30–40% of the asking price.
2. Walk away if the price doesn't come down — they'll often call you back.
3. Compare prices at multiple shops before committing.
4. Be friendly and smile — bargaining is social in Egypt, not confrontational.
5. Final price is usually 40–60% of the opening price.
6. Don't touch or show strong interest in an item before you're ready to negotiate.
7. Shopkeepers may offer tea — it's hospitality, not a commitment to buy.

## Dress Code

### General Guidelines
- Egypt is a conservative Muslim country. Modest dress is appreciated, especially outside tourist resorts.
- **Women:** Cover shoulders and knees in the street and in non-resort areas. Loose-fitting clothes are more comfortable in the heat anyway.
- **Men:** Long pants are preferred in Cairo and cities. Shorts are fine at beach resorts only.
- Swimwear: Only at pools and private beaches. Never walk around town in swimwear.

### Mosque Dress Code
- Women MUST cover: head (bring a scarf), arms, and legs. Long skirts or pants, long sleeves.
- Men: Long pants, covered shoulders. No shorts.
- Shoes off at the entrance (there's usually a shoe rack or someone to watch them).
- Some major mosques provide coverings at the entrance.

### Church Dress Code
- Similar to mosques: modest dress. Shoulders and knees covered.
- Women may need to cover their head in Coptic churches (varies).

## Ramadan
- During Ramadan, Muslims fast from dawn to sunset (no food, water, or smoking).
- **Restaurants:** Many are closed during the day. Tourist-oriented restaurants stay open but may be discreet about it.
- **After Iftar (sunset meal):** Cities come alive. Streets are bustling, shops open late, festive atmosphere.
- **Suhoor (pre-dawn meal):** Late-night eating culture, many places open until 3–4 AM.
- **Respect fasters:** Don't eat, drink, or smoke visibly in public during fasting hours. It's not illegal for tourists but it's disrespectful.
- **Business hours:** Often shortened. Government offices may close by 2 PM.
- **Best time to visit mosques:** Evening during Ramadan — beautiful atmosphere, taraweeh prayers.

## Common Greetings and Phrases

### Arabic Basics for Tourists
- Hello: "Ahlan" (أهلاً) or "Salam aleikum" (السلام عليكم)
- Thank you: "Shukran" (شكرًا)
- No thank you: "La shukran" (لا شكرًا)
- How much: "Bekam?" (بكام؟)
- Too expensive: "Ghali awi" (غالي أوي)
- Beautiful: "Gameel" (جميل) / "Gameela" (جميلة)
- God willing: "Insha'Allah" (إن شاء الله) — used constantly
- Good morning: "Sabah el kheir" (صباح الخير)
- Good evening: "Masa el kheir" (مساء الخير)
- Yes: "Aywa" (أيوا)
- No: "La" (لأ)
- Please: "Min fadlak" (من فضلك, to a man) / "Min fadlik" (من فضلك, to a woman)
- Where is...?: "Fein...?" (فين...؟)
- I don't understand: "Mish fahem" (مش فاهم)
- I don't speak Arabic: "Ana mabatkallimsh arabi" (أنا مابتكلمش عربي)
- The bill please: "El hesab law samaht" (الحساب لو سمحت)

## Common Scams and How to Avoid Them

### Scam 1: "The Museum/Site is Closed"
Someone near a tourist attraction tells you it's closed and offers to take you to their shop instead. **It's almost never closed.** Walk past them and check yourself.

### Scam 2: Inflated Taxi Fares
Always use metered white taxis or ride-hailing apps. If using a black taxi or tok-tok , agree on the fare before getting in.

### Scam 3: Unsolicited Tour Guides
People at the Pyramids, temples, or museums who start "helping" you without being asked, then demand payment. Politely say "la shukran" and walk away.

### Scam 4: "Free" Gifts
Someone hands you a souvenir, perfume sample, or papyrus "as a gift," then demands money. Don't accept items from strangers at tourist sites.

### Scam 5: Currency Confusion
Some vendors quote in "guineas" (جنيه) but then claim they meant dollars or euros. Clarify "Egyptian pounds" before any transaction.

### Scam 6: Camel/Horse Ride Bait-and-Switch
At the Pyramids: agree on price for a "30-minute ride," then the handler takes you far out and demands more money to bring you back. Only use licensed operators, agree on everything upfront, and keep your phone charged with GPS.

### Scam 7: "Special Price Because You're My Friend"
Flattery is a sales technique. The price is not special. Bargain normally.

### General Safety Tips
- Egypt is generally safe for tourists. Violent crime against tourists is rare.
- Petty theft (pickpocketing) exists in crowded areas — keep valuables secure.
- Use hotel safes for passports and excess cash.
- Avoid walking alone in unfamiliar areas at night.
- Women travelers: expect some verbal attention. Firm "la" and walking away is effective. Harassment is illegal and can be reported to Tourist Police (126).
