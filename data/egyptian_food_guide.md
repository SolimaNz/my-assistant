# Egyptian Food Guide — Local Restaurants and Street Food

## Must-Try Egyptian Foods

### Breakfast
- **Foul Medames (فول مدمس):** Slow-cooked fava beans with cumin, lemon, olive oil, and tahini. The national breakfast. Costs 15–40 EGP at a street cart.
- **Ta'meya (طعمية):** Egyptian falafel made from fava beans (not chickpeas like Lebanese falafel). Crispy outside, green inside. 5–10 EGP for a sandwich.
- **Shakshuka:** Eggs poached in spiced tomato sauce. Common at local cafes.
- **Feteer Meshaltet (فطير مشلتت):** Flaky layered pastry, sweet or savory. Like an Egyptian croissant. 30–80 EGP.

### Lunch / Dinner
- **Koshari (كشري):** THE Egyptian street food — rice, lentils, macaroni, chickpeas, fried onions, and spicy tomato sauce. A full meal for 25–60 EGP.
- **Molokhia (ملوخية):** Jute leaf soup served over rice with chicken or rabbit. A staple Egyptian home dish.
- **Kebab and Kofta:** Grilled lamb or beef. Best at local grill restaurants. 100–200 EGP per plate.
- **Hawawshi (حواوشي):** Spiced ground meat stuffed in bread and baked in a wood-fire oven. 30–60 EGP.
- **Shawarma:** Egyptian-style shawarma in bread (not wrap). Chicken or beef. 40–80 EGP.
- **Mahshi (محشي):** Stuffed vegetables — grape leaves, peppers, zucchini, eggplant — filled with spiced rice. A home-cooking specialty.
- **Fattah (فتة):** Layers of rice, bread, meat, and garlic-vinegar sauce. Traditional celebration dish.

### Desserts
- **Om Ali (أم علي):** Egypt's bread pudding — puff pastry, milk, nuts, raisins, baked until golden. 40–80 EGP at restaurants.
- **Konafa (كنافة):** Shredded pastry with cream or cheese, soaked in sugar syrup. 30–60 EGP per piece.
- **Basbousa (بسبوسة):** Semolina cake soaked in syrup. Sweet and dense.
- **Balah El Sham (بلح الشام):** Deep-fried churro-like pastry dipped in syrup. Available at most sweet shops.
- **Halawa (حلاوة):** Sesame halvah, eaten with bread for breakfast or as a snack.

### Drinks
- **Karkade (كركديه):** Hibiscus tea, served hot or cold. Very refreshing. National drink alongside tea.
- **Sahlab (سحلب):** Hot creamy milk drink with cinnamon and coconut, popular in winter.
- **Fresh Juice:** Mango, guava, strawberry, sugarcane — juice shops are everywhere. 15–40 EGP.
- **Egyptian Tea (شاي):** Very sweet black tea, served in small glasses. Offered everywhere.
- **Ahwa (قهوة):** Turkish-style coffee. Order "sada" (no sugar), "mazbout" (medium), or "ziyada" (extra sweet).
- **Sugarcane Juice (عصير قصب):** Fresh-pressed at street stalls, especially in Upper Egypt. 10–20 EGP.

## Famous Local Restaurants in Cairo

### Koshari
- **Abou Tarek** — 16 Champollion St, Downtown. THE most famous koshari in Egypt. Always packed. A large plate costs 40–55 EGP.
- **Koshari El Tahrir** — Near Tahrir Square. Good quality, local favorite.
- **Koshari Hilal** — Multiple branches. Consistent quality.

### Foul and Ta'meya
- **Gad (جاد)** — Chain with branches everywhere. Reliable foul, ta'meya, shawarma. Open very early for breakfast.
- **Felfela** — 15 Hoda Shaarawy St, Downtown. A classic since 1963. Tourist-friendly but authentically Egyptian.
- **Zooba** — 26th of July St, Zamalek. Modern Egyptian street food concept. Instagram-friendly but genuinely good.

### Grills and Kebab
- **Abou El Sid (أبو السيد)** — 157 26th of July St, Zamalek. Upscale traditional Egyptian. Beautiful decor. Mains 150–350 EGP.
- **Sobhy Kaber** — Multiple locations. Excellent kebab and kofta at reasonable prices. Very popular with locals.
- **Andrea** — Cairo-Alexandria Desert Road & Mariouteyya branch. Famous for grilled chicken in a garden setting. A Cairo institution.

### Seafood
- **Kadoura (قدورة)** — 19 Corniche El Nil (near Qasr El Nil Bridge). Pick your fish, they grill or fry it. Best waterfront seafood in Cairo. 200–400 EGP per person.
- **Fish Market** — Citystars Mall, Nasr City. Choose your fish by weight, pick the cooking style.

### Fattah and Traditional
- **Koshary Abou Hanafy** — Ramses area. Famous for their koshari and traditional dishes.

### Feteer
- **El Abd Bakery (العبد)** — Multiple branches across Cairo. Famous for pastries and feteer. The Tahrir branch is iconic.
- **Egyptian Feteer places:** Found all over — look for wood-fire ovens.

## Street Food Carts
- Found on almost every corner in Downtown Cairo, Giza, and popular areas.
- **Sweet potato carts (بطاطا):** Roasted sweet potato in winter. 10–20 EGP.
- **Corn carts (ذرة):** Grilled or boiled corn. 10–15 EGP.
- **Liver sandwiches (كبدة):** Spiced liver in bread. 20–40 EGP. Try "Kebdet El Prince" chain.
- **Termis (ترمس):** Lupini beans sold in cups as a snack. 10 EGP.

## Eating Out: Practical Tips
- Lunch is the main meal in Egypt (2–4 PM). Many restaurants are busiest then.
- Dinner is typically late (9–11 PM).
- Tap water is NOT recommended. Drink bottled water (widely available, 5–10 EGP).
- Wash fruits and vegetables with bottled water if eating raw.
- If your stomach is sensitive, avoid salads at street carts. Stick to cooked food.
- Most restaurants add a 14% VAT and may add a 12% service charge. Check the bill.
- Delivery apps: Talabat, elmenus (popular in Egypt).
