# Practical Information for Living and Traveling in Egypt

## Currency and Money

### Egyptian Pound (EGP / جنيه مصري)
- Currency code: EGP. Symbol: E£ or ج.م
- Denominations: 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200 EGP notes. Coins: 0.25, 0.50, 1 EGP (coins are rare in practice).
- Approximate exchange rate (subject to change): 1 USD ≈ 50 EGP, 1 EUR ≈ 55 EGP, 1 GBP ≈ 63 EGP.

### Where to Exchange Money
- **Banks:** Best rates, but slow (queues). CIB, QNB, Banque Misr have tourist-friendly branches.
- **Exchange offices (صرافة):** Faster than banks, competitive rates. Found in airports, malls, and tourist areas. Al Ahly Money Exchange and others.
- **Airport exchange:** Convenient but slightly worse rates. Exchange just enough for transport on arrival (~500–1000 EGP), then exchange more in the city.
- **Hotels:** Usually the worst rates. Avoid unless urgent.

### ATMs
- Widely available in Cairo, Alexandria, Luxor, Aswan, Hurghada, Sharm El Sheikh.
- Most accept Visa, Mastercard, and international cards.
- Withdrawal limits: typically 5,000–10,000 EGP per transaction.
- Fees: your bank's international fee + sometimes 20–50 EGP local ATM fee.
- Best ATM networks: CIB, QNB, Banque Misr, HSBC.
- Carry cash as backup — many small shops, taxis, and local markets are cash-only.

### Credit Cards
- Accepted at hotels, upscale restaurants, malls, and chain stores.
- NOT accepted at: street food, local shops, taxis, microbuses, many mid-range restaurants, markets.
- Visa and Mastercard are most widely accepted. Amex is limited.

## SIM Cards and Internet

### Getting a SIM Card
- Buy at the airport on arrival — all three operators have booths after customs.
- **Vodafone Egypt:** Largest network, best coverage. Tourist SIM packages available.
- **Orange Egypt:** Good coverage and competitive data prices.
- **Etisalat (e&):** Good in cities, slightly less coverage in remote areas.

### Tourist SIM Pricing (approximate)
- SIM card + registration: ~50–100 EGP
- 10 GB data (30 days): ~150–200 EGP
- 20 GB data (30 days): ~250–350 EGP
- Unlimited data packages: available from ~400–600 EGP/month

### Requirements
- Passport required for SIM registration (mandatory by law).
- Maximum 3 SIM cards per passport.
- Activation takes a few hours after registration.

### WiFi
- Available at most hotels, cafes, and restaurants (quality varies widely).
- Starbucks, McDonald's, Costa Coffee have reliable free WiFi.
- Internet speed in Egypt ranges from slow to moderate. Don't expect fiber speeds.

## Visa Information

### Tourist Visa
- **Visa on Arrival:** Available for citizens of most European countries, USA, Canada, Australia, and others. Purchased at the airport before passport control.
- **Cost:** $30 USD (single entry, updated March 2026) or $60 USD (multiple entry). Payable in USD cash (exact change preferred), EUR, or GBP.
- **Duration:** 30 days.
- **E-Visa:** Available at visa2egypt.gov.eg. Apply online before travel. Same price, avoids the airport queue.
- **Extension:** Possible at the Mugamma building in Tahrir Square. Complex process — budget a full day.

### Visa-Free Countries
- Citizens of Bahrain, Hong Kong, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Libya, Malaysia, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, UAE get visa-free entry for varying durations.

## Weather and Best Time to Visit

### Cairo and Lower Egypt
- **Oct–Mar (Winter):** Best time. 15–25°C days, 8–14°C nights. Can be chilly in December–January evenings.
- **Apr–Sep (Summer):** Hot. 35–45°C. Extremely uncomfortable for sightseeing. Avoid outdoor activities midday.

### Upper Egypt (Luxor, Aswan)
- **Nov–Feb:** Best time. Still warm (20–30°C) but bearable.
- **May–Sep:** Extremely hot. 40–50°C. Luxor and Aswan are among the hottest cities in the world.

### Red Sea (Hurghada, Sharm)
- Year-round destination. Water temperature: 22–28°C.
- **Best diving:** March–May and September–November.
- **Windy season:** March–May (great for kitesurfing).

### Alexandria and Mediterranean Coast
- **May–Sep:** Beach season. 25–32°C.
- **Dec–Feb:** Cool and rainy. 10–18°C.

## Electricity
- **Voltage:** 220V, 50Hz
- **Plug Type:** Type C (European 2-pin round). Some buildings also have Type F.
- **Adapters:** If coming from US, UK, or Australia, bring a universal adapter. Available at electronics shops in Egypt for 50–100 EGP.

## Health and Water
- **Tap water:** NOT safe to drink. Use bottled water for drinking and brushing teeth.
- **Bottled water brands:** Baraka, Nestlé Pure Life, Dasani, Hayat. Costs 5–10 EGP at shops, 20–40 EGP at restaurants and hotels.
- **Food safety:** Cooked food from restaurants is generally safe. Be cautious with raw salads, unpeeled fruits from street vendors, and ice.
- **Sun protection:** Use SPF 50+ sunscreen. Wear a hat. Egypt's sun is intense, especially in Upper Egypt and the Red Sea.
- **Dehydration:** Drink more water than you think you need. Carry a bottle at all times.
- **Travel health kit:** Bring anti-diarrheal medication (Imodium), rehydration salts, and basic pain relief.

## Photography Rules
- **Allowed:** Exteriors of most monuments, streets, public areas. Most museum interiors (without flash).
- **Not Allowed:** Military installations, bridges, government buildings, airports (interiors), some museum galleries.
- **Always Ask:** Before photographing people, especially women. Most Egyptians are happy to be photographed if you ask politely.
- **Extra Fees:** Some temples and museums charge for photography permits (50–300 EGP). Tripods and professional cameras often require special permits.
