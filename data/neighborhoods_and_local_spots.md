# Egyptian Neighborhoods Guide — Where Locals Actually Go

## Cairo Neighborhoods

### Zamalek (الزمالك)
- **Vibe:** Upscale island neighborhood on Gezira Island in the Nile. Tree-lined streets, embassies, art galleries, boutique cafes.
- **Best For:** Walking, cafes, restaurants, sunset Nile views, Cairo Opera House.
- **Where to Eat:** Zooba (Egyptian street food elevated), Sequoia (Nile-view lounge), The Bakery Shop (breakfast), Abou El Sid (traditional Egyptian upscale).
- **Where to Coffee:** Left Bank, Café Corniche, TBS (The Bakery Shop).
- **Don't Miss:** Cairo Tower (Borj al-Qahira) — 360° city views, 70 EGP entry. The Fish Garden (Grotto Garden). Walking along the Nile Corniche at sunset.
- **Getting Around:** Very walkable. Easy Uber access. Near Opera Metro station.

### Downtown Cairo (وسط البلد)
- **Vibe:** The chaotic, beautiful heart of the city. French colonial architecture, crowded streets, old-school coffee shops, and a gritty authenticity that's purely Egyptian.
- **Best For:** Budget travelers, authentic Egyptian street life, architecture lovers, nightlife.
- **Where to Eat:** Abou Tarek (best koshari), Felfela (classic Egyptian since 1963), Kazaz (late-night liver and sausage sandwiches).
- **Where to Coffee:** Café Riche (historic, since 1908), Groppi (iconic European-style café), any baladi coffee shop on Talaat Harb Street.
- **Don't Miss:** Tahrir Square, Egyptian Museum, Talaat Harb Street for walking, El-Borsa area for nightlife.
- **Metro:** Sadat Station (Lines 1 & 2 interchange).

### Maadi (المعادي)
- **Vibe:** Quiet, green, suburban. Popular with expats and families. Wide streets, old villas, lots of international restaurants.
- **Best For:** Families, long-term stays, peaceful walks, international food.
- **Where to Eat:** Lucille's (American brunch), Road 9 area (restaurants and cafes lined up), Korean and Japanese restaurants along the main roads.
- **Don't Miss:** Maadi's old streets (Roads 7, 9, 15) for walking. The Nile Corniche in Maadi is peaceful.
- **Metro:** Maadi Station, Sakanat El-Maadi Station (Line 1).

### Heliopolis (مصر الجديدة)
- **Vibe:** Historic neighborhood with art-deco buildings, wide boulevards, and a local Egyptian middle-class character. Recently renovated.
- **Best For:** Egyptian urban life, Baron Palace, local cafes.
- **Where to Eat:** Gad (chain, always reliable), local foul and ta'meya shops on every corner, Tikka (grills).
- **Don't Miss:** Baron Empain Palace (stunning Indian-style palace, now open as a museum, 120 EGP), Al-Orouba Street for coffee shops.

### Mohandeseen and Dokki (المهندسين والدقي)
- **Vibe:** Busy, commercial, dense. Lots of shops, restaurants, and nightlife along Arab League Street and Shehab Street.
- **Best For:** Shopping, street food, local Egyptian nightlife.
- **Where to Eat:** Anas El Demeshqi (excellent Syrian shawarma), Spectra (grills), endless local sandwich shops.
- **Getting Around:** Close to Behoos and Dokki Metro stations (Line 2).

### 6th of October City / Sheikh Zayed
- **Vibe:** New suburban satellite city west of Cairo. Malls, gated communities, wide roads.
- **Best For:** Mall of Egypt (ski slope inside!), modern restaurants, if you're staying in west Cairo.
- **Where to Eat:** Mall of Egypt food court and restaurants, Jurena (Egyptian), countless cafes in Beverly Hills and Sheikh Zayed compounds.

### New Cairo / 5th Settlement (القاهرة الجديدة / التجمع الخامس)
- **Vibe:** Modern, spread out, lots of malls and compounds. Cairo Festival City and Point 90 malls.
- **Best For:** Modern dining, shopping malls, AUC (American University in Cairo) campus.
- **Where to Eat:** Cairo Festival City restaurants, Ovio (Italian), Tabla Luna.

## Cafés and Ahwa (Coffee Shop) Culture

### What is an "Ahwa" (قهوة)?
An ahwa is a traditional Egyptian coffee shop — not a Starbucks-style café. It's where locals gather to:
- Drink tea (شاي) or Turkish coffee (قهوة)
- Smoke shisha (hookah/water pipe)
- Play backgammon (طاولة) or dominoes
- Watch football matches on TV
- Simply sit and watch the world go by

### Ahwa Etiquette
- Sit down and a waiter will come to you.
- Tea: 10–25 EGP. Coffee: 15–30 EGP. Shisha: 30–80 EGP.
- You can sit for hours — nobody will rush you.
- Tipping: round up or leave 5–10 EGP.
- Women are welcome at most ahwas, but some very traditional ones may be male-dominated. Use judgment.

### Famous Ahwas
- **Fishawi's (El Fishawi)** — Khan El-Khalili. Operating since 1773. THE most famous ahwa in Egypt. Touristy but atmospheric. Tea costs more here (~30–50 EGP).
- **Ahwa El Borsa** — Downtown, near the stock exchange. Intellectual/artistic crowd.
- **Ahwet El Nadwa** — Sayeda Zeinab area. Very traditional, very local.

### Modern Cafes
- **Cilantro** — Egyptian Starbucks equivalent. Multiple branches. Good WiFi.
- **The Loft** — Zamalek. Upscale, Nile views.
- **Café Corniche** — Zamalek. Popular study/work spot.
- **Beano's** — Multiple branches. Coffee and pastries.

## Alexandria Neighborhoods

### Corniche (الكورنيش)
- The waterfront promenade stretching along the Mediterranean. Perfect for walking. Fish restaurants line the eastern section.

### Anfushi and El-Gomrok
- Old Alexandria. Narrow streets, old houses, authentic seafood. Abu Ashraf and Kadoura for the best fish in the city.

### Montazah (المنتزه)
- Royal gardens and palace (Montazah Palace). Beautiful park on the sea. Entry ~25 EGP. Great for families and picnics.

### Stanley and San Stefano
- Modern waterfront area. San Stefano Mall, Stanley Bridge (iconic photo spot).
